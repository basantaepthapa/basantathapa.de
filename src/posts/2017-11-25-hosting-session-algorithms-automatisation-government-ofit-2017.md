On 23 November 2017, I hosted a discussion round about algorithms and automatisation in government at the annual conference of the [Kompetenzzentrum Öffentliche IT (ÖFIT)](http://www.oeffentliche-it.de). ÖFIT is a project at the [Fraunhofer Institute for Open Communication Systems](https://www.fokus.fraunhofer.de/en) that acts as a think tank on the uses of information technology in government and society, financed by the German [Federal Ministry of the Interior](https://www.bmi.bund.de/EN/home/home_node.html). Their annual conference 2017 dealt with the effects of algorithms and datafication on government, media and individual rights.

I was initially requested as a speaker, but then reshuffled to host the discussion. Instead, [Christian Djeffal](https://www.hiig.de/en/staff/christian-djeffal/) talked about guidelines for artificial intelligence in government, [Peter Parycek](https://www.donau-uni.ac.at/en/universitaet/whois/00570/index.php) outlined the impact of Big Data analytics on the policy cycle, and [Klaus Lenk](http://www.klauslenk.de/) placed the topic in a broader sociological perspective. They were later on joined on the discussion panel by two member of the Bundestag, [Saskia Esken](http://www.saskiaesken.de/) (SPD) and [Anke Domscheit-Berg](http://ankedomscheitberg.de/) (LINKE).

https://youtu.be/tFrGBthyXMA?list=PL766zD6HYeC\_ILZl-fzXyA2deVAQdQBes

Despite my questions, the discussion remained at a quite abstract level, which shows that the development is still in its early stages and that we need more discussions around concrete use cases.
